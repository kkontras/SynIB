## 0. Critical finding discovered during setup: the paper's PID-XOR M_Random / M_Learned never mask

`destroy_block(x, mask, block_list)` in `Xor_PID3Main_MaskSynIB_Search.py` silently returns the
input **unchanged** when `block_list` is not a list. Both the Fig. 7 sweep code
(`train_synib_mstar`, mrand/mlearned branches) and the Fig. 8 script
(`figs_training_dynamics.py`) call it with the int `1`. Consequently, in all published PID-XOR
results for M_Random and M_Learned, **the counterfactual KL was applied to intact inputs** — a
confidence penalty (KL-to-uniform on the clean fusion prediction, λ=10) — and the learned gates
from the inner loop were discarded unused.

Verified three ways (2026-07-25):

1. `x̃ − x = 0` on every batch for both branches (direct probe).
2. On CUDA, M_Random and M_Learned runs are **bitwise identical** (identical per-batch losses,
   weights, and final metrics; identity arm below shows S acc 0.859 for both, same per-seed
   values). The paper's 0.87 vs 0.89 difference is RNG-stream noise from its CPU runs.
3. The paper's cached artifacts (`synib_internal/artifacts/training_dynamics/pid_full.json`)
   were generated by this same code path (single commit in the internal repo).

**Scope:** confined to the synthetic PID-XOR scripts. The oracle M\* passes a real list
(`DESTROY_MASK=[MASK_SYN]`) and genuinely corrupts; Spurious-XOR (`xor_spurious_pub.py`) uses
`apply_destroy` (real); the benchmark trainer (`src/synib/models/vlm/synib_mask_model.py`)
applies masks correctly.

Because the published code path cannot answer the reviewer's question (nothing is masked), the
experiment below runs **two tracks**: *as-published* (no-op corruption, reproduces Fig. 8) and
*fixed* (`destroy_fix: true` passes `[1]`, so the sampled/learned mask actually corrupts). The
rotation question is answered on the fixed track.

### 3.1 The rotation effect is representation-level, not masking-level

Rotated arms score ~4 pp *higher* than identity arms for every KL-based variant. An 8-seed
expansion on the cheap cells confirms this is not seed noise, and that it appears identically in
a mechanism with **no masking at all**:

| Mechanism (same code both sides) | identity (n=8) | rotated (n=8) | diff |
|---|---|---|---|
| KL on intact inputs (no masking) | 0.868 ± 0.014 | 0.911 ± 0.005 | +4.3 pp (2.8 SE) |
| Random masking (real) | 0.876 ± 0.011 | 0.915 ± 0.006 | +3.9 pp (3.2 SE) |
| Oracle M\* / source-basis | 0.878 ± 0.012 | 0.920 ± 0.007 | +4.2 pp (3.1 SE) |

Vanilla is unmoved (0.501), so rotation alone fixes nothing; the effect is an interaction
between the KL regularizer and the input representation. Rotation is absorbable by the first
Linear layer (the function class is unchanged; per-coordinate SNR is invariant to the
post-rotation standardization), so the effect is purely about optimization geometry. Reading:
in the axis-aligned basis the scarce synergy signal occupies 6 dedicated coordinates that the
wide encoder can isolate and partially memorize even under the KL penalty (identity arms show a
low seed tail: 0.785–0.814); after rotation the synergy direction shares first-layer weights
with the abundant U1/R signals, so the rare slice rides on features trained and regularized by
the whole dataset (rotated arms are tighter and higher). Do NOT read Table 1's
identity-vs-rotated differences as a masking effect.

### 3.2 Nonlinear mixing arm (x ← tanh(Qx))

Since orthogonal rotation is absorbable by the first layer, we also ran an invertible
*nonlinear* mixing arm — elementwise tanh after rotation, before standardization
(`rotated_full_tanh.json`) — the strongest version of the reviewer's concern: the unimodal
support becomes a curved manifold. Pre-registered prediction (made before the run): no collapse,
since the mechanism does not use coordinate structure. Result (3 seeds):

| Method | Total acc | S acc |
|---|---|---|
| Vanilla | 0.947 ± 0.002 | 0.488 ± 0.018 |
| SynIB M_Random (real) | 0.858 ± 0.038 | 0.834 ± 0.014 |
| SynIB M_Learned (real) | 0.897 ± 0.024 | 0.874 ± 0.010 |

Prediction held: the task remains learnable (vanilla U1/R at 0.99), vanilla synergy stays at
chance, and both SynIB variants retain most of their synergy gains under nonlinear entanglement.
The cost shifts to the *unique* slice (masking + saturated features drop U1 to 0.75–0.82 mean).
The learned-vs-random ordering flips here (learned ≥ random), but learned remains destroy-all-like
throughout.

**Figure 1** (per-source train/val dynamics, Fig. 8 style, identity-fixed vs rotated-full vs
rotated-partial): `docs/figures/rebuttal_entangled_xor/fig1_entangled_dynamics.{pdf,png}`.

## 4. Experiment 2 — mask–oracle overlap (identity arm)

Per epoch, gates are learned on a fixed held-out val batch (64 examples) with the current model
at paper HPs; the mask's *kept* set (its implied unimodal support) is compared against the
ground-truth unique+redundant block positions (12 of 32 coords per modality). Baseline: a
Bernoulli(0.5) mask. Curves (per seed + mean, both modalities averaged):
`docs/figures/rebuttal_entangled_xor/fig2_mask_oracle_overlap.{pdf,png}`.

| Metric (final epoch) | Learned mask (paper HPs) | Learned mask (100 steps, λ_M=0) | Random mask |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard IoU | **0.0013** (0.0007 / 0.0013 / 0.0020) | **0.306** (0.299 / 0.298 / 0.320) | 0.274 |
| Soft AUROC (support ranking) | 0.58 late (0.74 at epoch 0) | 0.62 (stable all training) | 0.50 |
| Destroy fraction | 0.997 | **0.625** (0.625 / 0.628 / 0.623) | 0.50 |

Two-regime picture:

- **At paper HPs (20 inner steps, λ_M=1)** the *hard* mask does not discover the unimodal
  support: IoU is two orders of magnitude below the random baseline, flat across all 30 epochs;
  training gates destroy 99.6–99.8% of coordinates — the binarized counterfactual degenerates to
  destroying the entire modality (the M_Full control matches M_Learned in both arms: 0.866 ±
  0.057 identity, 0.873 ± 0.041 rotated). **However, the soft gate ranks support dimensions as
  more destruction-relevant than non-support dimensions above chance throughout training**
  (AUROC 0.56–0.59 late, and 0.73–0.76 at epoch 0) — the adversary *identifies* the dimensions;
  the 20-step budget plus binarization discards the signal. Same on the as-published track
  (`fig2_mask_oracle_overlap_as_published`), so this is not caused by the destroy fix.
- **With a longer inner loop (100 steps, λ_M=0; tuned arm, motivated by an isolated-adversary
  probe)** the hard mask genuinely localizes: IoU 0.31 > random 0.274 in every seed, AUROC 0.62,
  and the training-time destroy fraction converges to **0.625 — exactly the ideal complement of
  the unimodal support** (synergy+noise = 20/32 coordinates). Synergy accuracy: 0.876 ± 0.008
  (identity). Under rotation, where no axis-aligned support exists, the same configuration
  settles at destroy fraction 0.555 — random-mask-like — with synergy accuracy 0.889 ± 0.013,
  statistically close to the random-masking arm (0.915 ± 0.006). This is the paper's claimed
  fallback behavior, observed directly: *localize when structure exists, degrade toward random
  masking when it doesn't.*

Curves: `fig2_mask_oracle_overlap.{pdf,png}` (paper HPs, now incl. AUROC panel) and
`fig2_mask_oracle_overlap_steps100.{pdf,png}` (100-step arm).

**λ_M sweep (tuned arm — reported separately, pre-registered contingency).** Mild sweep
{0.5×, 1×, 2×} of λ_M = 1.0, M_Learned, 3 seeds:

| λ_M | identity S acc | rotated S acc | final IoU (identity) | destroy frac |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.816 ± 0.078 | 0.815 ± 0.065 | 0.002 | 0.99 |
| 1.0 (paper) | 0.862 ± 0.013 | 0.845 ± 0.046 | 0.001 | 0.99 |
| 2.0 | 0.890 ± 0.036 | 0.873 ± 0.013 | 0.001 | 0.99 |

Accuracy is flat within noise and the adversary stays saturated at every λ_M — the mild sweep
does not restore localization.

## 5. Pre-registered prediction checklist (filled honestly)

**(a) Random masking is rotation-invariant (rotated ≈ identity)? PASS (no degradation).**
Identity 0.876 ± 0.011 → rotated 0.915 ± 0.006 (n=8 each). Not merely invariant: it *improves*
by 3.9 pp (3.2 SE) — but Sec. 3.1 shows the same +4 pp appears for the no-masking KL mechanism
and the oracle, so this is a representation-level optimization effect, not a masking effect.
The pre-registered concern — degradation under rotation — did not occur.

**(b) Vanilla remains at chance on synergy under rotation? PASS.**
0.501 ± 0.013 (identity: 0.504 ± 0.011). U1 and R remain at 0.99+.

**(c) M_Learned under rotation — measurement, no pass/fail.**
Rotated M_Learned = 0.845 ± 0.046 (primary Q); 0.828 ± 0.024 pooled over 3 rotation seeds.
Identity-arm learned (real masking) = 0.862 ± 0.013, so the primary-Q mean sits within the
pre-registered "recovers ≥ identity − 2pp" band (0.845 vs 0.842) and the pooled mean sits
marginally below it. It does NOT degrade to the vanilla floor (0.50), converges ~2× slower, and
lands **below** random masking under rotation (0.915 ± 0.006, n=8; gap ≈ 9 pp on the pooled
learned mean) — and it is the only variant that fails to receive the +4 pp representation-level
lift of Sec. 3.1. Crucially, Experiment 2 shows the
pre-registered dichotomy's premise fails: the mask never localizes the unimodal support in
*either* arm (IoU ≈ 0.001 vs random 0.27), and M_Learned is indistinguishable from destroy-all
(M_Full) in both arms. Its rotation-insensitivity therefore reflects the rotation-insensitivity
of full-modality corruption + KL, not joint-training-induced localization.

**(d) IoU rises substantially above the random-mask baseline? Split verdict.**
*At paper HPs: FAIL* — hard-mask IoU 0.0013 vs 0.274 (random), flat across training, seeds,
modalities, and the mild λ_M sweep; the binarized mask saturates to destroy-all. *Soft ranking:
PASS* — the gate's soft scores rank the true support above chance throughout (AUROC 0.56–0.59
late, 0.74 at epoch 0). *With a 100-step inner loop (tuned arm): PASS* — hard IoU 0.31 > 0.274
in every seed, destroy fraction converges to the ideal 0.625, synergy accuracy 0.876 ± 0.008;
under rotation the same configuration reverts to random-like masking (destroy 0.555, acc
0.889 ± 0.013). The mask *can* figure out the dimensions; the paper's 20-step budget discards
that signal at binarization.

## 7. Honest bottom line (for internal use)

1. **The reviewer's specific claim fails on this benchmark, but vacuously.** Neither rotation
   nor nonlinear tanh-mixing collapses any SynIB variant — because nothing in the pipeline
   actually exploits axis-aligned structure: the learned mask degenerates to destroy-all, and
   the published variant didn't mask at all. Rotated arms actually score ~4 pp *higher* for all
   KL-based variants — a representation-level optimization effect (Sec. 3.1), present even with
   no masking, absent for vanilla; do not attribute it to masking.
2. **The paper's Fig. 7/8 M_Random/M_Learned numbers are a confidence penalty**, not masking
   evidence, and should be corrected (fix is one character: `[1]`). With the fix, headline
   conclusions survive (all variants ≈ 0.85–0.91 vs vanilla 0.50), but M_Learned no longer beats
   M_Random anywhere on PID-XOR, and under rotation M_Random is significantly better.
3. This is consistent with the E2 benchmark finding (learned ≈ random across all 3,494
   endpoints) — the pattern is now mechanistically explained on the synthetic task: the inner
   adversary saturates at paper HPs (λ_M ∈ {0.5, 1, 2} all saturate; cf. the `lsparse` idling
   note from the mask-collapse analysis). The refined story: **the adversary identifies the
   dimensions (soft ranking above chance at paper HPs; full hard-mask localization with a
   100-step inner loop, destroy fraction converging to the ideal 0.625) but the 20-step budget
   + binarization discards the signal**, so accuracy-level gains at paper HPs are carried by
   the KL objective. The 100-step arm also demonstrates the graceful fallback directly:
   localized masking on identity, random-like masking under rotation.
4. If the rebuttal cites this experiment, cite the **fixed** numbers and the source-basis oracle,
   and frame W5 as answered by rotation-invariance of the *objective*, per R1 above.
